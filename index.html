RHEED pattern simulator

Kinematic model: reciprocal-lattice rods cut by the Ewald sphere, projected onto a flat screen.

FerroThinFilms Lab · Department of Physics and Astronomy · University of Nebraska–Lincoln · xiaoshanxu.unl.edu

Screen pattern

3D Ewald construction (drag to rotate)

How to use
  • Set the beam energy, incidence angle and azimuth, then define the surface lattice with a, b and γ, or pick a preset. Both views update as you move a slider.
  • The left view is the pattern on a flat phosphor screen (horizontal axis y, vertical axis z). The brightest yellow spot is the specular (0,0) rod, and the horizontal line at the bottom is the shadow edge.
  • The right view shows the Ewald construction in three dimensions: x is the in-plane direction of the beam, y is in-plane and perpendicular to it, and z is the surface normal. Drag to rotate.
  • At azimuth 0 the real-space vector a lies along the beam, and b is at angle γ from a. The azimuth rotates the lattice about the surface normal.
Model

The incident wavevector has magnitude k = √(2mE)/ħ and makes the angle θ₀ with the surface. The surface lattice gives reciprocal-lattice rods perpendicular to the surface, at in-plane positions G = n₁a* + n₂b*. Elastic scattering requires the outgoing wavevector to lie on the Ewald sphere of radius k, so every rod that cuts the sphere gives one diffracted beam. The beam direction (kx, ky, kz) is projected onto the screen plane x = 1.5 k, giving the spot position (y, z) = (1.5 k·ky/kx, 1.5 k·kz/kx). The screen distance only rescales the pattern. Spot size follows sin(θ₀)/sin(Θ/2), where Θ is the angle between the incident and diffracted beams; this is a qualitative weighting, not a calculated intensity.

Limitations

This is a kinematic, qualitative tool. The rods are infinitely thin, so there is no streaking from finite domain size, roughness or film thickness. It has no Kikuchi lines, no inner-potential refraction and no dynamical effects, so spot intensities are only indicative. The wavelength is non-relativistic, which changes k by roughly 1% at 20 keV. The screen is flat and perpendicular to the in-plane beam direction.
